Performance

ConditionalLogit.jl compared with R, Stata and Python

Seven synthetic scenarios were fitted, from 40,000 to 1,000,000 rows. With default settings and one thread, ConditionalLogit.jl was:

  • 7–22× faster than R survival::clogit(method = "exact");
  • 13–41× faster than Stata clogit on one core;
  • 144–324× faster than Python statsmodels ConditionalLogit.

The "best" comparison uses each program's fastest multi-core configuration: Stata/MP on 16 cores, and ConditionalLogit.jl on 8 or 24 threads. On that basis ConditionalLogit.jl was 18–53× faster than Stata/MP. All three programs give the same coefficients, up to their convergence tolerances (see Agreement).

Same stopping point

The three programs stop at different points:

  • ConditionalLogit.jl stops by default at the SAS GCONV = 1e-8 iterate.
  • R stops when the relative change in ℓ is below 1e-9.
  • Stata starts from its own initial values and stops when the scaled gradient is below 1e-5 (e(ic) counts its iterations).
  • statsmodels uses BFGS and stops when the gradient norm of −ℓ/n is below 1e-5. It does not report an iteration count.

The table below repeats the one-thread comparison with ConditionalLogit.jl fully converged (gconv = 1e-16). That is more accurate than either R or Stata, so this is the conservative comparison: 6–19× faster than R, 11–49× faster than Stata on one core, and 125–385× faster than statsmodels.

The differences from Stata come from Stata's default stopping rule, not from the likelihood. Refitting match1to4 in Stata with nrtolerance(1e-12) tolerance(1e-14) shrinks the difference from 2e-7 to 4e-10. The same holds for R on panelfe, where R stops a little early. statsmodels' default BFGS stops much earlier, at about 1e-5 (see Python statsmodels); with method="newton" it converges to the same estimates.

Method

  • What is timed: a complete fit from data already in memory, through each program's usual interface. That covers grouping, the design matrix, all iterations and the covariance matrix. Reading the CSV file is not timed.
  • Repetitions: R and Stata give the median of 3 fits. statsmodels also gives the median of 3 fits, except where a single fit takes longer than 30 s, which is timed once. Its times varied by 10–30% between two runs. ConditionalLogit.jl gives the median of 5 fits after one untimed fit that compiles the code. Times under 0.1 s are noisy at the millisecond level.
  • Settings: each program's defaults. R and statsmodels are single-threaded. Stata/MP was run with set processors 1 and with all 16 licensed processors.
  • Precision: Stata reads the CSV files with import delimited, asdouble. Without asdouble it stores the data as single-precision floats, and its estimates then differ in about the 8th digit.
  • Host: Intel Core i9-14900HX (8 performance and 16 efficiency cores, 32 hardware threads), Linux, Julia 1.13.0, R 4.6.1 with survival 3.8-6, Stata/MP 15 (16-core licence), Python 3.13.14 with statsmodels 0.15.0.

Interpretation

  • One thread: most of the gain comes from the kernels. Strata with one event use a centred softmax, and the general case uses the log-space recursion described in Algorithm. The cost is O(nk · min(mk, nk − mk) · p²) per stratum, and the per-stratum work allocates nothing.
  • Threads: threading helps most when the per-stratum work is heavy (multi, panelfe, bigstrata: 2.4–4.4× from 1 to 24 threads). With one event per stratum, the fixed, partly serial cost of grouping and building the design dominates, so the gain is small (choicelarge: 1.5×). On this host, going past the 8 performance cores can slow the smallest problems down.
  • statsmodels runs its recursion in pure Python, one group at a time, which accounts for most of its gap.
  • Stata/MP gains up to 3.3× from 16 cores on the heavy scenarios. On pairs it is slightly slower with 16 cores than with 1.
  • Absolute times depend on the machine. The ratios show the difference between the implementations on this benchmark; they do not predict the speedup on every data set.

Reproducing the benchmarks

julia --project=benchmark/compare -e 'using Pkg; Pkg.instantiate()'
julia --project=benchmark/compare benchmark/compare/scenarios.jl         # writes data/*.csv (≈ 570 MB)
Rscript benchmark/compare/r_bench.R                                       # needs survival, data.table
uv venv benchmark/compare/.venv && uv pip install --python benchmark/compare/.venv/bin/python statsmodels pandas
benchmark/compare/.venv/bin/python benchmark/compare/py_bench.py
for t in 1 8 24; do julia --project=benchmark/compare -t $t benchmark/compare/julia_bench.jl; done
(cd benchmark/compare && stata-mp -b do stata_bench.do)                   # Stata 15 or later
julia --project=benchmark/compare benchmark/compare/report.jl            # Markdown tables

The raw results behind the tables above are in benchmark/compare/results/.

Package-internal benchmarks

benchmark/bench.jl times the individual stages (design build, one evaluation of ℓ, score and information, and full fits through the matrix and formula interfaces), and benchmark/run_threads.jl sweeps thread counts and checks that the results are bitwise identical. See Development.
